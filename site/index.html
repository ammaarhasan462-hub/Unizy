How it works

Three steps. About ten minutes.

At A-level you had past papers and mark schemes. At UCL you get the papers but nothing to check your answers against. SaveMyGrade fills that gap.

  1. 01

    Pick your module

    Search all 6,682 modules in UCL’s catalogue by code, title or department.

  2. 02

    Answer a question

    Choose one of our exam-style questions, or paste one from your own past paper. Set a 45-minute timer if you want exam conditions.

  3. 03

    See your class

    Marked against your department’s criteria, with feedback on each criterion and the 3 changes that would move you up a class.

Why not just use a chatbot?

A chatbot doesn’t know how your department marks. SaveMyGrade does. Every answer is marked against UCL’s grade bands for your year and, where your department publishes them, its own grade descriptors.

See what a First looks like

Every marked answer comes with a First-class answer to the same question to compare against. Model answers written by students who got a First in the module are being added, with their name and mark shown.

Questions

Good to know

Is this cheating?

No. SaveMyGrade is for exam practice and revision. It refuses to mark coursework you’re going to submit, and it never writes work for you to hand in.

Does it have my exact past papers?

We don’t host university exam papers. Paste a question from your own past paper and we’ll mark your answer to it, or use one of our exam-style questions written from the module’s official description.

How accurate is the marking?

It’s a practice estimate, so you always see a mark range rather than a single number. We test the marker against real marked answers module by module, and you can tell us your real mark to help keep it accurate.

Which modules?

All 6,682 modules in UCL’s public module catalogue, undergraduate and master’s. Practice questions are written the first time someone opens a module, then saved for everyone.

Is SaveMyGrade part of UCL?

No. SaveMyGrade is an independent revision tool built by a UCL student. It isn’t affiliated with or endorsed by UCL.
