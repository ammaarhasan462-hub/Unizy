Inside Unizy

Five things to do. Everything else stays out of your way.

The dashboard is organised around five actions. Extra tools, like essay marking and group chats, are there when you want them.

1

Learn

Your modules, week-by-week topics from the official outline, and a notebook for each. Press “Finished” after a lecture and Unizy queues practice and schedules reviews before your next deadline.

2

Practise

Topic tests at five difficulty levels, with mark schemes and model answers. Practice marking for essays and long answers.

3

Revise

Flashcards for every topic, plus cards made from your notes. Spaced repetition brings each card back just before you’d forget it.

4

Plan

Your classes, deadlines and revision sessions in one week view. Weak topics from your tests become suggested sessions.

5

Ask

An AI tutor whose answers show their sources: your module’s outline, Unizy library notes, or general AI knowledge flagged for you to check. Share card sets, group quizzes and seminar prep with friends at your uni.

How students use it

What it looks like for real assignments.

These are the actual workflows in Unizy, step by step. They’re examples of how the tools fit together, not stories from named students.

An essay due in two weeks

  1. Open the module in Learn to see which topics the question draws on.
  2. Ask the tutor to help you plan an argument and find counter-arguments.
  3. Paste your draft into Assess practice: it’s marked against UK degree grade bands with what to improve.
  4. Fix the weakest criterion, then mark it again.

Assess gives practice guidance, not an official mark. Never submit AI-written work as your own.

Weekly problem sets

  1. Take a topic test on this week’s topic at “Exam standard”.
  2. Read the model answer for anything you dropped marks on.
  3. Weak topics are flagged automatically in Revise and Plan.
  4. Ask the tutor to walk you through a similar problem.

After every lecture

  1. Press Finished on that week’s topic in Learn.
  2. Unizy suggests the matching flashcards to review tonight.
  3. Practice questions you haven’t answered yet go into your practice queue, and anything you didn’t follow is saved to ask the tutor.
  4. Reviews are added to your Plan after 1 and 3 days, and the day before your next deadline.

Seminar prep with friends

  1. Post a seminar-prep list to your group chat: one reading or question per line.
  2. Everyone ticks what they’ve prepared, so you can split the reading.
  3. Run a group quiz on the topic and compare scores.
  4. Tag the tutor for anything the group is stuck on. Its answers show their sources.

Your notes stay private unless you choose to share a copy with a group.

Exam revision

  1. Upload your lecture notes and turn them into flashcards.
  2. Review the cards that are due each day: spaced repetition handles the timing.
  3. Mixed-topic tests show what’s still weak.
  4. Add revision sessions for those topics to your Plan, around your deadlines.
Does it actually help?

Built on the study methods with the strongest evidence, and measured on our own students.

Making notes and flashcards isn’t the point. Remembering more in less time is. So Unizy is built around two methods with decades of research behind them, and it measures whether they’re working for each student.

The research behind it

Testing yourself beats rereading.

Students who practised recalling a text remembered more of it a week later than students who spent the same time rereading it.

Roediger & Karpicke (2006), Psychological Science
Spacing reviews out beats cramming.

A review of hundreds of experiments found that spreading study over time leads to better long-term memory than the same study in one block.

Cepeda et al. (2006), Psychological Bulletin
These two methods top the rankings.

A major review of ten common study techniques rated practice testing and spaced practice as the most useful, and rereading and highlighting among the least.

Dunlosky et al. (2013), Psychological Science in the Public Interest

In Unizy: topic tests and flashcards are practice testing; the review schedule, lecture check-ins and planned reviews are spaced practice.

What’s covered

Honest about what’s covered, and what isn’t yet.

Unizy lists every module from each university’s official 2026/27 course pages. How much ready-made revision material a module has depends on its coverage level, and every module in the app shows its level.

Module pack

Notes, flashcards and questions written for that exact module from the university’s official syllabus, then fact-checked by a separate reviewer. We’re starting with every compulsory first-year module.

Subject library

The module is matched to a Unizy subject library with notes, flashcards and test questions. The material is written for the subject, not from your lecturer’s slides.

Closest library

There’s no exact library for this module yet, so you get the closest Unizy library for your degree. Your own notes and AI-made cards fill the gaps.

Outline only

No library material yet. You still get the official outline, your notebook, your own cards and the AI tutor, but no ready-made tests.

Each module in the app shows which of these it is, so you always know what you’re getting. Your university’s own materials (lecture slides, reading lists and past papers) are still the final word.

Trust & safety

Where the information comes from, how the AI is checked and how your data is kept safe.

Where the academic information comes from

  • Modules, credits, terms and topics come from each university’s official course pages, regulations and module catalogues for 2026/27. Each module links to its official page.
  • Library notes, flashcards and questions were drafted with AI from those outlines and standard academic sources, then checked in separate review passes against published sources (statutes, case law, textbooks, guidelines). Errors found were corrected.
  • Unizy is independent. No university wrote, checked or endorsed this material.

How AI answers are checked

  • The tutor is given your module’s official outline and the matching library notes, and told to answer from them.
  • Every answer lists its sources underneath: the official module outline, the Unizy library note it used, your own notes, or general AI knowledge, shown in amber so you know to check it. Each answer links to the official module page.
  • It’s instructed never to invent cases, statutes, studies, statistics or quotations, and to say when it’s unsure. It doesn’t give drug doses.
  • Marking uses UK degree grade-band criteria and is practice guidance, not an official mark.
  • AI can still be wrong, and answers aren’t checked by a person before you see them. Check important points against your lectures and reading. If you spot a mistake, use “Report a mistake” in the app and we’ll fix the library.

How your data is protected

  • Accounts and saved work are stored in London (Supabase, eu-west-2). Database rules lock each account’s data to that account.
  • No advertising, no tracking cookies, and we never sell your data. Site visits are counted as anonymous daily totals, with no cookies or IDs.
  • Your notes are private. Nothing goes to a group chat unless you choose to share it, and you can remove what you’ve shared.
  • The AI provider (Anthropic) doesn’t use what you send through its API to train its models by default.
  • Stripe handles payments; we never see your card details. You can ask us to delete your account and everything in it at any time.

Questions

Good questions to ask before you sign up.

Is Unizy free?

Yes. Notes, flashcards, topic tests, planning and friend groups are free. The free plan includes 10 AI actions and 1 Assess marking a week. Unizy Pro unlocks the full AI tutor and marking, and every new account gets 7 days of Pro free with no card. See pricing.

Do I need an account?

No. You can explore Unizy and save work on your device without one. An account saves your work across devices and is needed for the AI tutor, AI marking and friend groups.

Is Unizy connected to my university?

No. Unizy is independent and isn’t affiliated with or endorsed by any university. It uses universities’ published course information to set up your modules.

What if my module isn’t fully covered?

Every module shows a coverage label: Module pack, Subject library, Closest library or Outline only. Where coverage is thin, upload your own notes: Unizy can turn them into flashcards, and the tutor can work from the official outline.

Is the AI always right?

No. It’s grounded in your module’s outline and the checked library notes, and told to flag uncertainty, but it can still make mistakes. Treat it like a study partner, not an authority, and check important points against your lectures and reading.

Can I use Unizy to write my essays?

Unizy is built for learning and practice: planning, feedback and testing yourself. Follow your university’s rules on AI and don’t submit AI-written work as your own.
